## ABSTRACT 

Serving large language models at long context is bottlenecked by the key-value (KV) cache, which is read in full at every decode step. Attention keys are locally low-rank though globally high-rank: shared low-rank bases discard page-specific directions that a page’s own compact basis retains. L OCKS gives every page its own spectral summary (resident, about a tenth the cache’s size), reconstructs within-page logits, estimates each page’s attention mass by log-sum-exp, and attends only the top pages; selection itself reads no candidate keys or values. Selecting on this summary alone stays within about a point of the full cache on long-document QA (LongBench-v1), tracks the read-every-key oracle on retrieval-dense RULER down to the smallest budgets, and shows its largest margins on long-form reasoning (AIME26, MATH-500), where baseline selectors collapse. At its shipped 2048 -token budget L OCKS matches FullKV aggregate quality at 100 K+ context while attending about 2% of the tokens, and halves per-token decode latency ( 2.0× at 1M tokens) against dense attention. L OCKS 

ships as a drop-in plugin for unmodified vLLM, with batched decode running in full CUDA graphs. 

## 1 Introduction 

Decoding a token requires reading both the model’s weights and the sequence’s key-value (KV) cache, and which one dominates depends on the serving regime. At short context and low concurrency a step is weight-bound, since the cache is small and attention a minor fraction of it; production serving increasingly runs at the opposite end, where the weight read amortizes across a growing batch while the cache does not, since every request reads its own. Memory binds independently of speed. For Llama-3.1-8B with bf 16 KV, a 128 K context occupies roughly 16 GiB of KV cache per request, more than its own weights, so the cache, not the weights, caps how much context and how many requests an accelerator can serve. KV capacity and KV-read bandwidth are distinct bottlenecks. L OCKS targets the latter: it keeps the full KV cache resident but avoids reading most of it at each decode step; reducing resident capacity is complementary and left to future tiering or offload. Decode attention is nonetheless sparse. A few pages carry almost all of a query’s attention mass at each step, and attending only those pages recovers most of the full-cache output [ 46 , 52 , 74 ]. Exact mass reads every key; selection therefore needs a resident summary. Attention keys are locally low-rank though globally high-rank: a page’s own rank-8

eigenbasis captures most of its key energy, making it a high-fidelity compact surrogate for the page’s attention mass, so reconstructed-LSE selection from a tenth-of-KV resident summary tracks the read-every-key oracle down to the smallest budgets, because the carrier pages that decide tasks survive. Representation scope decides what a summary can preserve. A fixed projection shared across pages has provably unavoidable page-content blind directions; at page scope, rank 8 and a tenth of the bytes retain the oracle’s selection, and we measure the chain from page-local spectral concentration through logit and ranking fidelity (Fig. 1a,c) to carrier survival (Fig. 1b) and task quality (Fig. 1d). A page summary is worth only what selection can read back from it. Envelopes preserve extremes, the per-page min and max a query can align with [ 52 ]; block moments preserve spread, a centroid and a spread quadratic over the page’s keys [ 54 ]; HiLS learns a surrogate for the chunk log-sum-exp directly [ 26 ]. Existing summaries either preserve only coarse statistics, reuse a representation across pages, or require a trained surrogate. The open question is the scope at which that representation is shared. Loki uses a shared PCA basis calibrated offline [49 ]; ShadowKV constructs a sequence-level low-rank representation [ 51 ]. Neither scope is where the structure lives (§3). A fixed projection shared across pages is provably blind to page-specific directions (Prop. 1), and broadening a shared basis to the same or greater per-page state still does not recover the fidelity of the page-local factor (§3). A page-local representation must still be cheap enough to deploy. Per-page and per-sequence bases have so far mostly lived in research forks of the serving stack, or required training a gate onto the model [ 17 ]. L OCKS ships as a drop-in attention-backend plugin for unmodified vLLM that builds each page’s basis from one small eigendecomposition when the page completes and replays its batched decode step inside full CUDA graphs; each GQA group folds its queries’ page-mass shares into one shared selection through a single share-average rule, one fused kernel scores and ranks every page (§4, §5). This is one chain, named once here: page-local spectral concentration ⇒ token-logit fidelity ⇒ LSE ranking fidelity 

⇒ carrier survival ⇒ task quality at aggressive sparsity ⇒ practical end-to-end acceleration. Each link can fail; §3 exhibits rival summaries failing at different links, and the page-local representation is the one for which every link holds, theoretically through attention-output error (Thm. 1) and empirically to task quality (§5). We adopt exact page-level log-sum-exp mass as the established value-blind selection target (Lemma 1, App. A.1). LOCKS makes three contributions: • Locality finding and matched-state evidence. We show that the geometry relevant to page selection is concentrated at page scope rather than sequence or cache scope. This gap persists after matching representation state (§3). • Shared-projection impossibility and local construction. We prove that every fixed shared low-rank linear projection has page-content blind directions (Prop. 1), and introduce a per-page spectral summary whose unquantized form carries a composed bound from reconstruction error to coverage and attention-output error (Thm. 1); the deployed quantized summary’s fidelity is measured rather than bounded (§5.4). • Quality and deployability. We show oracle-tracking quality under aggressive budgets and integrate L OCKS 

into unmodified vLLM, obtaining long-context end-to-end acceleration (§5).

The knob is a fixed per-head token budget b (page budget k = ⌈b/B ⌉; the always-kept sink page and most recent page fill two of the k slots, so they are counted within b,2A PREPRINT - J ULY 28, 2026 not added to it), held constant as the context grows: unlike a fixed fraction, it provisions the exact, context-independent cache a serving system must allocate. Coverage governs sparse decoding’s output error. For a page set S, let γS (q) be the fraction of total attention mass that falls in S, and let oS be attention restricted to S with the softmax renormalized. Selection precedes the value fetch in this regime, and the identity below pins down the optimal rule for it. Retained mass controls the output error of restricted attention. Dropping a page set’s complement changes the attention output by exactly ε(¯ vSc − ¯vS ), with ε = 1 − γS (q)

and ¯vS , ¯vSc the mass-weighted mean values of the kept and dropped sets. The top-k pages by exact log-sum-exp mass maximize this retained mass, and among deterministic rules that do not read the values {vi} this ranking is worst-case optimal (Lemma 1, App. A.1). The exact identity is due to Tzachristas et al. [55] and the value-agnostic mass-criterion it implies is derived concurrently by Tian et al. [54] ; we claim neither as a contribution here, only the target they establish. Computing that exact mass, though, reads every key, the very traffic sparse decoding exists to avoid, so deployable selection needs a compact resident summary. The identity’s error term is exact, not just bounded. Shedding interchangeable bulk ( ¯vSc ≈ ¯vS ) is benign at any coverage, while shedding a single distinctive carrier token realizes the penalty near its ceiling however little mass that token carries. Exact page mass remains the selection target. Carrier-page recall is an empirical diagnostic: small average mass errors can conceal consequential ranking errors on the few task-critical pages, and the task collapses of §5 are the downstream consequence; §3 measures the split directly (full microscope in App. B). LOCKS stays value-blind; among deterministic rules that do not read the values, Lemma 1(ii) shows mass-ranking is worst-case optimal. Exact mass, however, reads every key (precisely the traffic sparse decoding exists to avoid), so selection needs a resident summary, and §3 takes up what such a summary must preserve to still find it. 

## 3 Representation Scope Determines Selection Fidelity 

§2 fixes exact log-sum-exp mass as the target and carrier-page recall as the diagnostic that average errors can hide; whether a resident summary preserves what the target needs is a question of scope , the set of pages it is fit to, not its size. Attention keys are locally low-rank though globally high-rank. A page’s own eigenbasis captures most of its within-page key energy at the shipped rank, while a per-sequence basis (ShadowKV [ 51 ]) captures a fraction of that and a single cache-wide basis (Loki [ 49 ]) almost none (Table D.11, App. D). The pattern is a dose-response that survives a matched-bytes control (Table D.11, matched-bytes rows). Grown to L OCKS ’s own per-page state or beyond, shared bases still trail the page-local basis on both mass and recall (Table D.15, App. D; Fig. 1a,c), which supports representation scope, rather than state size, as the source of the gap. Broadly shared low-rank bases lose page-specific directions, and increasing their rank or state does not recover the selection fidelity of page-local factors at comparable cost. Interpolating scope with position-bucketed shared bases degrades fidelity monotonically from page toward sequence scope, with recall, the carrier-side quantity, falling fastest (Table D.16). Two objections deserve pre-emption before the necessity question. First, a 16 -token page ( B=16 , App. D) has at most 

B − 1 = 15 nonzero modes once centered, so an unbounded pool of structure was never on offer; measured against synthetic flat-spectrum (isotropic) pages at the same rank, real pages concentrate well above this isotropic null (App. D), so the concentration reflects genuine local structure rather than an artifact of the mode-count cap. Second, the per-page fit is not overfitting. The basis is never asked to generalize beyond the cloud it was built from, since the summary only ever scores the very page it summarizes, so a page-specific fit is exactly the right inductive bias, not a statistical liability. The concentration is a property of the keys themselves. It holds whether a basis is fit before or after rotary position embedding is applied, and page scope pays essentially no rotation penalty (Table D.16, App. D). Proposition 1 makes the failure structural rather than quantitative: a fixed shared projection carries zero information about a (d − ρ)-dimensional family of within-page content differences, so for summaries of the stated form the first link of §1’s chain does not merely degrade on that family, it vanishes. 

Proposition 1 (Shared scope is content-blind) . Fix any W ∈ Rd×ρ with ρ < d and any scoring rule whose value on a page depends only on the query, the page centroid μ, and the sketched keys {W⊤ki}. For every unit u ⊥ span( W),every page P with at least two keys, and every c > 0, the page P ′ obtained by adding c u to one key of P and −c u

to another receives the same score as P for every query, while for every query with q⊤u > 0 the exact log-masses separate without bound, sP ′ (q) − sP (q) → ∞ as c grows (proof in App. A.2). The envelope statistics of decode-time selectors admit the analogous statement (Lemma 2). 

The statement covers shared linear sketches with a centroid, the class instantiated by the global- and sequence-basis baselines considered here; extreme-based bounds receive the same conclusion through Lemma 2, while codebooks and second-moment cores fall outside the linear-sketch hypothesis and are handled empirically instead, via the frontier table of App. D (Table D.11). Proposition 1 applies once the shared basis W is fixed; adaptive procedures that refit W as 3A PREPRINT - J ULY 28, 2026 the underlying cache changes fall outside this impossibility statement and are evaluated empirically through the scope frontier instead. Rivals break the chain at different links, not merely with worse constants. At the smallest budgets the envelope (Quest [ 52 ]) tracks the read-every-key ceiling (exact LSE, Table B.1, App. B) on raw mass yet loses the pages that carry the answer, giving up little total mass against the ceiling on both models while keeping the carrier less than half as often (Fig. 1b; Table B.1, App. B). Link 3 (LSE ranking fidelity) fails while the aggregate the envelope does preserve, total captured mass, stays nearly intact. The codebook [ 66 , 72 ] outranks the per-sequence basis on mass and recall, though captured energy is not even defined for it, since it is not a linear subspace (Table D.11). Captured energy is not what saves a rival, so it is not the quantity the chain actually needs. The moment core (COBS [ 54 ], concurrent work, and SPLA [ 56 ]) is exact for a Gaussian page, matching the page’s second-order statistics term for term, yet has its weakest worst-case control on the high-dynamic-range, peaky pages a carrier lives on (App. A.5). Link 2 is token-logit fidelity, strictly more than second-order energy. Each rival family breaks at least one link in the chain, though not necessarily the same one; the page-local representation is the one for which every link holds (Thm. 1 through attention-output error; §5 to task quality). §4 builds the construction that exploits this, one basis per page, and composes it into the retention guarantee (Thm. 1). 

## 4 LOCKS : Every Page Its Own Basis 

LOCKS keeps the full cache and selects, at every decode step and per KV head, the pages to attend under the fixed per-head token budget b of §2, from a resident per-page spectral summary of attention mass. It is training-free and applies to standard GQA decoder models. Exact mass (Lemma 1) would read every page’s keys, exactly the traffic sparsity exists to avoid, so L OCKS scores from a resident summary instead; that summary cannot be a shared projection, since a fixed, query-independent basis is provably content-blind (§3, Prop. 1), so L OCKS gives every page its own factorization.

Implementation. LOCKS loads into an unmodified vLLM 0.24 [ 29 ] as a pip-installable general plugin , a drop-in attention backend that registers itself with the engine, no fork or patch required (App. F). ## 5 Evaluation: Measuring Every Link 

We test quality at a fixed attended budget and decode efficiency (§5.3), both under the fixed per-(layer, KV-head) token budget b of §2. Every quality figure sweeps b over the shared grid b ∈ { 64 , . . . , 2048 } against FullKV, the exact-LSE oracle (Lemma 1), L OCKS , and a suite of state-of-the-art selectors, evictors, and reasoning compressors named per benchmark (§5.1, §5.2). All methods run in the same engine against the same FullKV reference, on identical records. Per-arm sampling settings are stated with the per-task tables of App. C. The full protocol, model roster, and budget accounting are in App. C. 

5.1 Retrieval and long-document QA 

Retrieval and long-document QA are the regime where selection must find few, specific pages. L OCKS tracks the read-every-key oracle (exact-LSE selection, Lemma 1) down to the smallest budgets and stays within about a point of the full cache on LongBench-v1 [ 1], while four state-of-the-art selectors and evictors break away as the budget shrinks (Fig. 2), namely Quest [ 52 ], KVzip [ 28 ], ShadowKV [ 51 ], and RocketKV [ 2 ]. The gap is widest on retrieval-dense RULER [ 25 ] at the smallest budgets, where carrier-page retention decides the answer (Fig. 1b; §3, App. B). At 100 K+ context on InfiniteBench (GLM-4-9B-Chat-1M [ 53 ]) the same ordering holds: L OCKS tracks the exact-LSE oracle and achieves the highest measured mean of any deployable baseline at both a tight and a generous budget (Table 1); the full per-task grid across all budgets is in App. C. 

5.2 Long-form reasoning 

Reasoning decodes thousands of tokens, so selection runs over a cache the model itself is writing, and the working set is roughly fixed in size rather than a fraction of the context. Quality holds only once the budget covers it, so the sweep reveals a per-task budget floor (Fig. 3). The read-every-key oracle marks that floor for value-blind selection, and L OCKS ’s gap to the oracle isolates estimation fidelity from it, while four state-of-the-art baselines (Quest, R-KV, TriAttention, and LazyEviction) trail across the sweep. 5.3 Efficiency 

LOCKS keeps the cache resident on the accelerator, so its footprint is the full cache plus the summary, traded for less per-step read traffic, since decode attends only the selected pages. We measure decode efficiency on an H200 NVL node, where FA3 is native. Every number here is measured in vLLM 0.24 on the H200 NVL node (App. C.6, F); kernels and CUDA-graph capture ship with the code. At a budget where selection matches the full cache on the quality suites of §5.1 and §5.2, L OCKS reads far fewer KV bytes per decode step than a dense engine and turns that into lower per-token latency, adding only the one-time summary build to time to first token; both gaps widen with context (Fig. 4). Batching decode rows amplifies the gap. As more requests share the step, the dense engine’s per-row cache read comes to dominate while L OCKS ’s selected read does not, so the speedup grows with batch size at every context (Table 2).

Eviction and selection before the query. Discard entries before the query that will use them exists, namely attention-frequency heuristics [ 33 , 74 ], sink-and-window structure [ 60 ], adaptive head budgets [ 16 ], reconstruction-scored eviction [ 28 ], and synthesis into compact surrogates [ 77 ]; DuoAttention keeps a full cache only for retrieval heads, giving streaming heads a constant-length window instead [ 61 ]; and R-KV [ 4] evicts redundancy-scored entries during 

reasoning decode, committing periodically rather than once. 

Query-aware sparse attention and what a selector stores. Keep the full cache, attend a subset each step. Static patterns fix the subset by position [ 3, 10 , 70 ]; query-aware selection chooses it per query. What organizes the family is 

what a selector stores per page , namely rank-0 statistics, extremes, centroids, or representative keys [ 24 , 51 , 52 , 59 ]; a shared global or offline basis [ 30 , 32 , 43 , 49 ]; codebooks [ 66 , 72 ]; block moments [ 54 , 56 ]; or scores and votes at token or head granularity [ 37 , 40 , 58 ]. HiLS [ 26 ] trains a landmark surrogate for the chunk log-sum-exp [ 42 ]. Twilight and Tactic [ 35 , 75 ] estimate masses toward a coverage target; TidalDecode [ 65 ] instead cuts the cost of selection itself by reusing one layer’s ranking across others; Prism [ 57 ] selects blocks spectrally; and several further directions cheapen sampling, retrieval, or prefill sparsity [8, 27, 36, 46]. 

Theory of sparse decoding. An exact error identity for top-k attention truncation [ 55 ] underlies this line of work; Expected Attention [ 14 ] estimates per-token attention mass in closed form from the query and key distributions; VATP [ 19 ] weighs importance by value norm rather than attention weight alone. vAttention [ 12 ] unifies top-k with sampling under statistical guarantees; BLASST [ 68 ] instead bounds error via a global softmax-mass threshold rather than a fixed top-k budget; VaSE [7] argues for value-aware stochastic eviction. 8A PREPRINT - J ULY 28, 2026 

Quantization. Keep every token, reduce bits per entry [ 20 , 22 , 23 , 39 , 63 ], orthogonal to which tokens are attended. Runtime-certified bounded-error quantized attention [5] bounds the resulting output error. 

Low-rank and architectural. Grouped-query and multi-query attention reduce the number of distinct key/value projections cached in the first place [ 48 ]. Low-rank compression and graded-precision storage change what is stored, not which pages are read [ 6, 31 , 47 , 76 ]. Guarantee-flavored kernels and two-stage selectors [ 2, 9 , 13 ] target the same exact-top-k ceiling that a resident summary approaches more cheaply. 

Trained sparsity and hybrids. The frontier increasingly bakes efficiency into pretraining, trained sparsity, or linear/recurrent attention alike [ 11 , 45 , 69 , 71 ], needing pretraining control; SeerAttention [ 17 ] instead distills a sparse-attention gate onto an already-pretrained model. HARD-KV [ 67 ] adapts per-head compression during decoding. A reasoning-specific line targets the budget under long chain-of-thought traces. R-KV [ 4] evicts redundancy-scored entries during reasoning decode, ReasonAlloc [ 38 ] allocates the budget hierarchically across the reasoning trace, and LongFlow [ 50 ] estimates importance from an intermediate attention quantity as the trace grows. Adaptive Mass-Segmented KV Compression [ 64 ] instead imposes region-wise quotas over long reasoning traces and is complementary to the per-page mass estimator studied here. 

Concurrent work. The exact truncation identity appears independently in [ 55 ] and [ 54 ], with the latter also deriving the value-agnostic mass criterion; SPLA [ 56 ] instead trains a second-order metric to select blocks; and Prism [ 57 ]selects blocks spectrally. L OCKS ’s per-page exact-logit reconstruction, its shared-projection impossibility (Prop. 1), and its composed retention guarantee (Thm. 1) are the elements that distinguish it within this concurrent cluster. 

## 7 Conclusion 

Attention keys are locally low-rank though globally high-rank: a page’s own rank-8 eigenbasis captures most of its key energy, making it a high-fidelity compact surrogate for the page’s attention mass, so reconstructed-LSE selection from a tenth-of-KV resident summary tracks the read-every-key oracle down to the smallest budgets, because the carrier pages that decide tasks survive. We measured every link in this chain, and each held. Page-local spectral concentration carried through to logit fidelity, ranking fidelity, carrier survival, task quality under aggressive sparsity, and end-to-end serving throughput. The resulting selector, L OCKS , ships as a training-free plugin for vLLM, its decode step running fully inside CUDA graphs. 

Limitations. Selection ranks summaries directly. The resulting error is characterized for the unquantized summary (Thm. 1); the deployed quantized summary’s retention is measured empirically, not certified per step. The summary costs about a tenth of the cache; the page size is fixed at 16 . Because selection reads only the resident summaries and never the keys or values, a fully offloaded serving path that keeps only summaries on the accelerator is a natural extension we leave to future work. The page-level measurements of App. B expand a small number of records per model, characterizing the estimator in depth rather than establishing coverage across tasks and models. 

Scope of the claims. Our optimality claims are class-relative. Mass-ranking is minimax-optimal among deterministic value-blind selectors (Lemma 1, App. A.1), and the spectral basis is residual-optimal among orthogonal rank-r

per-page linear reconstructions (Cor. 1); neither reaches past that class. L OCKS is empirically strongest among the evaluated deployable summaries at matched state (§3, App. D). Two directions complement rather than compete with ours. Better mass estimators help where a deterministic summary cannot (vAttention [ 12 ] unifies top-k with sampling under statistical guarantees), refining how mass is estimated at the cost of the reads our summary avoids, whereas we refine what each page stores .

Reproducibility statement. All proofs are in App. A; the evaluation protocol and budget accounting are in App. C; code, kernels, container stacks, and the measurement harnesses are released (App. F).

A.2 Content-blindness and the local spectral basis (Prop. 1, Lemma 2, Cor. 1) Envelope bounds are content-blind. A cheap surrogate must still distinguish pages by content, which the extreme-based bounds decode-time selectors rank by cannot do. 

Lemma 2 (Envelope content-blindness) . The envelope score of Tang et al. [52] , divided by √d, upper-bounds every within-page logit, but depends on a page only through its per-dimension key extremes: pages with equal extremes receive identical scores for every query. 

Pages that differ only in identifiers, exactly where selection decides the answer, receive identical envelope scores, and no tie-break recovers the distinction; at extreme budgets the envelope loses carrier pages, not just average mass (Table B.1). ## B The Microscope: Selection Fidelity and Estimator Error 

Tables B.1 and B.2 come from a single instrumented decode harness, released with our code (App. F). Queries and cached keys are captured exactly as attention consumes them (a scaled_dot_product_attention hook at decode steps; keys from the KV cache, verified bit-identical to what the kernel received), during greedy decoding of five RULER-32K records ( niah_single_2 , niah_multikey_2 , niah_multiquery , vt , qa_1 ) per model; every generated answer was correct, so the captured queries are genuine retrieval-regime decode queries. Statistics are computed in fp64 per (record, layer, query head, decode step) over pages of B=16 keys; ground-truth carrier pages come from the answer strings’ token spans. The same instrumented traces also produce the measured-error, rotation-control, and registry files reported elsewhere in the paper (Table B.2; App. D). Over these traces the harness also verifies the proven bounds numerically, finding on every audited page zero violations of the envelope bound (Lemma 2), the outlier inequalities (Lemma 3), or the reconstruction bound of Prop. 2. 

Small mass errors, large carrier-ranking errors. Table B.1 separates the two failure modes. Both are measured against exact-mass selection itself, whose conditional carrier retention is total by construction, so every deficit is estimator error rather than a mischosen target. The envelope gives up little of the ceiling’s mass yet collapses on the carrier pages that decide tasks at the smallest budgets, the mechanism behind the task-score collapses of envelope selection reported in §5.

C.1 Per-method budget accounting 

A nominal b does not buy the same number of reads in every method, so a comparison at equal b is not a comparison at equal cost. The four baselines differ as follows. • Quest. Selects independently per QUERY head, so the pages a GQA group reads are the union over its G

heads: at most Gb tokens, at least b, with G=4 for Llama-3.1-8B. The upper bound is reached when the G

heads select disjoint pages. Every other arm here selects per KV head and therefore pays no such union. • ShadowKV. Selects per KV head via the group max, so no union, but the always-attended outlier block ( 48 

chunks of 8) and local window ( 32 tokens) are a fixed 416 tokens that we count inside b. Actual selection is therefore 96 , 608 and 1632 tokens at b = 512 , 1024 and 2048 . At b ≤ 256 the fixed block does not fit, the chunk solve returns zero selected chunks, and the method degenerates to dense; we do not report those points. • RocketKV. Spends b as decode TRAFFIC rather than residency, split evenly between the attention half and the top-k estimator, so attended tokens are b/ 2 and measured traffic is 0.77 to 1.00 × nominal. Its budget does not bound the resident cache, which stays at prompt plus generated. • KVzip. Bounds only the retained CONTEXT. It never evicts the question or the generated turn, so decode reads are b + |q| + g with g the tokens generated so far: about 2.4× nominal at b=64 , and 1.05 × at b=2048 .It is also the one arm run at max_num_seqs = 1 : its selection is bound outside the attention path through a single active kept-set, so a second concurrent decode row would read another record’s selection. Every other arm runs at max_num_seqs = 4 .The shared axis is b tokens per (layer, KV-head) at page size 16 , with the always-kept sink and most-recent pages counted inside b, never added on top (§2). Each port lives in src/<method>/ , with the run wiring in benchmarks/registry.py and vllm/integration/runner_common.py ; every cell below is produced by 

benchmarks/run.py , which refuses to record a sparse cell whose measured sparse-decode activation is not 1.00 on every decode row. 

Budget counting. Every method’s per-(layer, KV-head) token budget b is counted the same way. The always-kept sink and most-recent pages fill two of the k = ⌈b/B ⌉ page slots, counted within b, never added on top of it (§2). C.2 Reasoning baselines (MATH-500, AIME26) 

The reasoning suite adds three decode-time eviction baselines, each a parity-gated faithful port, using the published configuration where compatible with the shared budget; any budget-mapping deviation is stated explicitly below. R-KV and LazyEviction are approximately residency-matched to b, though LazyEviction meters its budget per query head, so its resident set carries the GQA-group factor G detailed in its entry below. TriAttention is run in its native prompt-protected reasoning configuration; therefore b controls only its generated-region selection and its actual resident/read state can exceed the nominal budget. The shared always-kept sink and recent pages are counted inside b as elsewhere. • R-KV [ 4 ]. Redundancy-aware eviction for reasoning: each token is scored by importance (attention mass) and non-redundancy (feature dissimilarity to already-kept tokens), mixed by λ, with a fixed retained buffer plus a recent-token buffer. We run the published reasoning config ( λ=0 .1, buffer 128 , retain ratio 0.2; an earlier draft used λ=0 .07 /buffer 64 , which our config audit corrected to the reported values). It is a strong reasoning-specific baseline. • TriAttention [ 41 ]. Trigonometric KV compression: keys are scored per query head via a complex-domain (real, imaginary, magnitude) query-mean functional, reduced by the max over each GQA group, and the top pages are selected while the full prompt and a local window are always retained. Our port protects the entire prompt span and selects only over the generated region with the published HF-reasoning selection order; TriAttention reports outperforming R-KV on long reasoning, which reproduces here. • LazyEviction [73 ]. Lagged eviction for reasoning: prefill stays dense, and during decode it tracks each token’s maximum recurrence interval (the longest lag between successive steps at which a query head attends to it) and evicts only once every W decode steps, at which point it retains the highest-importance tokens (softmax attention above threshold α) up to budget and always keeps the W most recent tokens, so a token re-attended on a lag survives an intervening lull. We run the published reasoning config ( α=10 −4 on Qwen3-4B, 5×10 −4

on Llama; a single decode path, parity-gated against the official implementation). The published absolute recent-window ( 363 tokens at the published 1492 capacity on Qwen-AIME) does not fit our tighter budgets, so we hold W at its published window-to-capacity ratio ( ≈ 0.243 ) across the shared 64 –2048 grid rather than at a fixed absolute size; this ratio-mapping is a harness convenience to keep W below the budget, not an official knob. Its budget is metered per query head , so its resident set is not directly comparable to a per-KV-head method’s b without the GQA-group factor G, the same residency-accounting caveat that applies to the other decode-time arms here. Because all three commit their eviction irreversibly during decode, once a chain-relevant token is dropped it cannot be recovered: at tight b the model loses coherence and generates to the length cap (a ≈ 2× decode-cost inflation, measured on our cells), which is why their accuracy collapses while L OCKS , which retains the selection state and re-selects exactly each step, tracks the read-every-key oracle and degrades far more gracefully. 24 A PREPRINT - J ULY 28, 2026

C.6 Decode efficiency: measurement protocol and full numbers 

Figure 4 in §5 reports decode efficiency as a banner; this section states the measurement protocol in full and gives the underlying numbers with dispersion, so that no cell is read as a single lucky draw. 

Configuration. All cells are batch size 1, tensor parallel degree 1, on one H200 NVL GPU ( sm_90a ), model GLM-4-9B-Chat-1M ( 40 layers, 4 KV heads, head dim 128 , page size 16 , bf16 ), at the shipped budget of 2048 tokens per (layer, KV head). The dense controls are FlashAttention-3 (FA3) and FlashInfer (FI); L OCKS runs its deployed kernel set (the packed rank-8 int-4 page score, the fused selection, the co-scheduled Q/KV projection, and the tail-refresh graph). Every cell is verified to have executed the deployed path and nothing else: the engine log must carry the 

[locks] ACTIVE and score=r8i4 banners and must not carry a fallback marker, else the cell is discarded rather than published. FA3, not FI, is the reference for the parity claim, because L OCKS delegates prefill to FA3; comparing against FI would fold in the FA3-versus-FI prefill kernel gap. 

What one cell measures. A cell first runs a warm prefill and a clock-ramp decode that absorbs one-time costs (kernel JIT, FlashInfer autotuning, CUDA-graph capture) and detects a stable SM clock; only then does it run one measurement prefill followed by three consecutive windows of 64 decode steps on the same live batch, for 192 timed per-token steps. Requests are aborted rather than finished, so the decode metric is not contaminated by end-of-sequence effects. Two guards protect each window: a lockstep check (every step must emit exactly the batch’s worth of tokens, since KV pressure can silently shrink the batch) and a throttle check (windows whose NVML sample shows a real hardware slowdown are dropped); a cell with zero clean windows is refused outright. Time-to-first-token (TTFT) is measured separately with three repeated prefills. That series is bimodal: the single high sample is the first prefill that follows a request which actually ran decode steps, and it carries the one-time summary build; the remaining samples are prefill-only. We report the low-sample mean as the steady TTFT and the high sample as the build-inclusive first token. 

Error semantics. TPOT is the mean over the 192 timed steps, reported as mean ± standard deviation; the coefficient of variation is 0.5–1.7% for the dense backends and 2.5–4.0% for L OCKS , the extra variance being the per-step selection and the periodic page-crossing refresh. Steady TTFT is the mean over all prefill-only repeats ( 9–20 per rung at 

16 K– 256 K, two per boot); its standard deviation is 5–280 ms on a 0.6 to 50 second prefill (under 0.6% ), so prefill timing is essentially exact and the dispersion column is omitted for it. The build column is reported as mean ± sd over 

8–10 boots per rung at every context ( 16 K– 1M; the bimodal TTFT series yields exactly one build-inclusive draw per boot). It is a genuinely heavy-tailed one-time cost, dominated by first-request allocator and cache effects rather than by the O(L) summary work itself, so at the shorter rungs its standard deviation is comparable to its mean and its profile across context is non-monotone (the wide 128 K reading is one boot’s stall in the tail: its median, 2.5 s, sits below the 

2.9 s mean). We report the full dispersion at every rung, 512 K and 1M included ( n=8 boots each), rather than hide the noise; the build does grow with context overall ( 0.5 s at 16 K to 3.7 s at 1M), consistent with its O(L) page-summary work. 

Reading the table. The decode saving grows with context, because the selected budget is a fixed 2048 tokens while the dense cache it replaces grows linearly: L OCKS attends and reads a nearly constant amount per step while the dense backends read the whole cache. Below the crossover near 30 K tokens the per-step selection and the always-attended summary cost slightly more than the attention they remove, so L OCKS is at parity or marginally slower there (the 

0.96 –0.98 × rows); from 64 K onward it wins, reaching 2.02 × the faster dense backend at 1M. Steady TTFT is at parity at every context: L OCKS does not touch the prefill kernel, and the two steady columns agree to within their 33 A PREPRINT - J ULY 28, 2026 few-millisecond noise. The only first-token cost L OCKS adds is the one-time summary build, which is a substantial fraction (roughly half) of the first token at 16 K but falls to about half a percent by 1M, where prefill itself is quadratic; in absolute terms it is a small one-time cost that grows only slowly with context ( 0.5 s at 16 K to 3.7 s at 1M, its O(L)

page-summary work) against a prefill that spans 0.6 to 699 seconds. 

Batched ( bs > 1) decode (Table 2). The batched numbers extend this protocol to concurrent serving. For each (backend, context) we boot one engine and sweep the batch size on a single live batch : one warm prefill and clock-ramp, then the same 192 -step windowed measurement – with the identical lockstep and throttle guards – at each batch size in turn, with no re-prefill between them. L OCKS and the two dense controls (FA3, FI) are therefore measured matched-pair in the same session at every context, and the reported speedup is L OCKS against the faster dense backend per cell (the conservative choice), the ratio of L OCKS ’s decode throughput to that backend’s at fixed batch. ## E Decode Kernel: Critical Path and Overlap Schedule 

This section gives a high-level picture of what one L OCKS decode step does on the GPU, and where the two design choices that keep it at or below dense TPOT live. L OCKS decode is two stages – score-then-select (Stage A) and sparse paged attention (Stage B) – but the win is as much about what is kept off the step’s critical path as about the stages themselves. Figure E.1 shows the schedule for one attention layer. 

The critical path. Stage A projects the decode query ( ht → q), scores every resident page by contracting the query with that page’s rank-8 int4 summary, and keeps the k highest-scoring pages per KV-head (the union table, §A.4). Stage B then runs paged attention over only those k pages. Everything on this path is fixed-shape and allocation-free, so the whole step is captured in one CUDA graph. 

(i) Splitting the QKV GEMM. A fused QKV projection would place the KV projection on the critical path even though the newly written token is not what Stage A scores. We instead split the projection: the Q-GEMV stays on the path (the score needs q), while the KV-GEMV, its RoPE, and the cache write are issued as a separate kernel that runs concurrently with the selector . The selector is intentionally barrier-bound and occupies only a handful of SMs, so the KV projection executes on the idle remainder and its latency is hidden rather than added. This is the schedule the deployed kernel set uses for non-QK-norm architectures at context ≤ 64 K; for QK-norm models (per-head q/k norm) the rope-absorbing variant is disabled and the projection reverts to a standard fused form (§C). 

(ii) Building the summary off the hot path. The page summary is a function of the page’s keys alone, not of any decode query, so it is built once when a page is finalized during prefill (or on a page-crossing refresh), not per decode step. The decode step reads the resulting int4 basis and never pays the eigendecomposition. This is what lets Stage A cost a compact-summary scan rather than a re-projection of the full keys, and it is the reason L OCKS touches the prefill kernel not at all (TTFT parity, §C). 40 A PREPRINT - J ULY 28, 2026

LOCKS is released as a pip-installable vLLM general plugin (pip install locks-kv ), a drop-in attention backend that registers itself with an unmodified engine, no fork or patch required (§4). Source code, the Hopper kernels, and the measurement harness behind App. B are available at https://github.com/Js-Hwang1/locks.git .

Software. Every number is measured in vLLM 0.24 .0 on PyTorch 2.11 .0 (cu130 ), CUDA 13 .0 runtime (cuBLAS 13 .0.0) under a 13 .2 driver, with a bfloat16 KV cache, full CUDA graphs, and prefix caching off. Every cell runs inside one pinned container image, NGC PyTorch 25.08-py3 (Singularity), so the toolchain is identical across all measurements.
